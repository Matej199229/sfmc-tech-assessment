Name beneficiaries for each Manulife group savings plan
If you have more than one plan i.e. a RRSP, DPSP or TFSA, you need to name a beneficiary for each plan. They can be the same person but designating a beneficiary for one doesn’t apply to all of them.
 
Follow the rules about who can be your beneficiary
You can name your spouse, children, another family member, a friend, or an entity, for example a charity, as a beneficiary. And if you name more than one, you decide how they share the money or property you leave for them. But remember, there are laws that might change your plans.
If you want to name a minor as your beneficiary, you should also name a trustee. In general, a trustee is a person or organization that manages someone else’s property or money. In this case, the trustee would manage the money you leave to your minor beneficiary according to the instructions under the trust.
 
 
Understand the types of beneficiaries
There are two types of beneficiaries, revocable and irrevocable.
  1. Revocable beneficiary means you can change your beneficiary at any time without getting their consent.
  1. Irrevocable beneficiary means you need their consent to change your beneficiary. In Quebec, if your spouse (civil union or married) is your beneficiary, they’re irrevocable unless you specify otherwise.
